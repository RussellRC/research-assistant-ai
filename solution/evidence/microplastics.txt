## Research Findings

Microplastics (MPs), defined as plastic particles under 5 millimeters, represent a pervasive and complex threat to marine ecosystem health. Their ubiquitous presence, coupled with their environmental persistence and capacity to interact with marine organisms across various trophic levels, results in a spectrum of adverse effects. These impacts stem from both the direct physical presence of the particles and the array of chemical contaminants they carry.

Physically, microplastics are readily ingested by marine organisms, from microscopic zooplankton to larger marine mammals. Once ingested, MPs can inflict physical damage to digestive tracts, induce false satiation leading to reduced nutritional intake and potential starvation, and obstruct feeding apparatuses. This physical interference has been shown to reduce feeding rates and reproductive success in studies involving zooplankton, highlighting a direct impact on individual organism viability.

Chemically, microplastics are not inert; they inherently contain additives like plasticizers and flame retardants which can leach into the environment or directly into organisms. Crucially, their hydrophobic nature and high surface area-to-volume ratio make them potent adsorbers of persistent organic pollutants (POPs), heavy metals, and pathogens from surrounding seawater.
